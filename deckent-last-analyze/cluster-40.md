## Summary
This cluster is the provider control-plane, and the dominant signal is **dead-on-arrival capability code shipping alongside a trust-without-verify runtime path**. Three otherwise-complete modules are entirely unwired — `provider-capabilities.ts` (zero importers), `provider-overflow.ts` (its only consumer chain, `handleRateLimitFailover`, has no callers), and `core/rate-limiter.ts` (a third, orphaned `RateLimiter` while two live copies in `api/` do the real work) — meaning real features (capability-based routing, subs→API overflow, per-tenant rate limits) exist in source but never execute. The live detection path meanwhile makes soft guarantees: `bootstrapProviders` runs a health check whose results are discarded (`provider.ts:1025`) and equates `<cli> --version` success with an authenticated session (GAP-4, `provider.ts:391`), the exact gap that the well-built `provider-auth-probe.ts` was written to close but which is only wired into `doctor`, not bootstrap. The lone stale-constant defect is `provisioner.ts:101` advertising "Node.js >= 18 (22 recommended)" against the ADR-001 Node-24+ floor. Net: deckent's provider layer has more capability surface than it actually wires, and its remaining live guarantees (health, auth, concurrency) are advisory rather than enforced.
